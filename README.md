## What you're buying

Supergoop sells Unseen as an invisible, weightless, scentless sunscreen. The full product name on Sephora is "Unseen Sunscreen Invisible Broad Spectrum SPF 40 PA +++", which tells you most of the specs in one line: broad-spectrum UVB and UVA coverage, SPF 40, PA+++ on the Japanese UVA scale (roughly PPD 8 to 16).

It's a chemical filter formula, not a mineral one. That distinction matters if you're shopping for reactive or rosacea-prone skin, because the mineral version is a separate product in the same family (more on that below). Several reviews of Unseen get this muddled, including one widely syndicated comparison that describes the dermatologist's comments about zinc oxide and titanium dioxide while also calling Unseen a chemical sunscreen. Both can't describe the same bottle. If a mineral filter is a hard requirement for you, Unseen isn't it.

What the formula does well, based on published testing: it disappears. No white cast, no tint, no fragrance, water- and sweat-resistant per the brand's own description. It also behaves like a primer. A Business Insider reviewer who compared it against a $9 dupe noted that makeup "glided on as if I was wearing a thin layer of moisturizer" and seemed to last longer over it.

What it doesn't do well is stay put through a workout. The same reviewer found that midway through a 30-minute session it felt like it was sliding off, and needed a towel and a reapply. Worth knowing before you buy it as your gym or pool sunscreen.

## Does the difference show up in real testing?

Partly. One published head-to-head against Trader Joe's $9 daily facial sunscreen found both were oil-free, fragrance-free, and clear on the skin, and the reviewer still preferred the cheaper one. Their specific complaints about Unseen: skin looked oily within minutes despite the oil-free marketing, eyes tingled for the first half hour after application, and it didn't survive a sweaty workout. Their specific praise: it didn't cause breakouts, makeup sat better on it and lasted longer, and skin felt less dry by the end of the day than usual.

That's one person's face, not a verdict. The parts that generalise are the ones tied to the formula: a clear finish, a good makeup base, decent comfort on dry skin, and limited sweat resistance. The parts that don't generalise are the oiliness and the eye sting, which are as much about your skin and how close you apply to the lash line as about the product.

If you're comparing on cost alone, the dupe comparison is legitimate and the price difference is roughly four to one. If you're comparing on texture and how it wears under foundation, that's where the premium is doing actual work.

## Questions people ask before buying

**Does it leave a white cast?** No. That's the entire premise of the product, and it holds up in testing.

**Is it mineral?** No. It's a chemical filter formula. The Mineral Unseen Sunscreen SPF 40 at $40 is the mineral option.

**Can I use it instead of a primer?** Reviewers say makeup applies smoothly and lasts longer over it. It's not a marketed primer, so treat that as a bonus rather than a guarantee.

**How long does a 50 mL tube last?** Around 40 face-and-neck applications at the standard quarter-teaspoon dose, less if you're reapplying properly every two hours outdoors.

**Do I need to reapply it?** Yes, every two hours during sun exposure, and after swimming, heavy sweating or towelling off. The formula's limited sweat resistance is the main reason.

**Is the SPF 50 worth paying more for?** SPF 40 blocks roughly 97.5% of UVB and SPF 50 blocks around 98%. The gap is small. Texture and whether you'll actually wear it matter more than that half a percent.
